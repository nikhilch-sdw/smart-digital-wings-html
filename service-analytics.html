Client FAQs

Common Questions About Our Analytics & CRO Service

Clear explanations of server-side data, tracking compliance, and A/B testing.

What exactly is server-side tracking and why is it better than client-side?
Standard client-side tracking sends data from the user's browser directly to Google or Meta, making it vulnerable to ad blockers and browser privacy rules (like Apple's iOS ITP). Server-side tracking sends data first to your own cloud server, which then securely sends verified purchase data to ad platforms—recovering up to 40% of lost signals.
Will server-side tracking slow down our website?
No, it actually speeds up your website! Because heavy third-party tracking scripts are moved from the user's browser to your cloud server, the visitor's device downloads significantly less JavaScript, resulting in faster Core Web Vitals and lower bounce rates.
How much traffic do we need to run meaningful A/B split tests?
For landing page or checkout A/B testing, we generally recommend a minimum of 8,000 to 15,000 monthly unique visitors and at least 150-200 conversions per month to reach statistical significance (95%+ confidence) within 2 to 4 weeks.
Can you integrate our offline sales and phone calls into GA4?
Yes. We set up measurement protocol webhooks that automatically send offline CRM conversion values (such as completed contract signings, in-store purchases, or qualified sales calls) back into Google Analytics 4.
How is our data protected and are your systems GDPR / DPDP compliant?
Yes, 100% compliant. Server-side tracking gives you complete control over what data is sent to third parties. We automatically hash and scrub all personally identifiable information (PII) before transmission to adhere strictly to international and Indian data protection regulations.
What will our Looker Studio dashboard look like?
You receive a custom-designed, single-pane-of-glass executive dashboard that brings your ad spend, ROAS, cost per lead, organic search traffic, and net store revenue into a clean visual interface with zero technical jargon.
